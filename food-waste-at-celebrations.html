Celebrations are about generosity, and generosity often means more food than anyone can eat. This page looks at how much food is lost at celebrations, why it happens, and what hosts, caterers and guests can do about it.

What that means for celebrations

Weddings and parties often order in bulk and in a hurry. A single mistake in quantity or a dish nobody wants can waste more than the rest of the meal combined.

Waste is also not just about leftovers. Ordering, portioning and serving decisions made days earlier matter just as much as what happens on the night.

Cook for the people you have

Household waste in the study was highest in some group sizes and was often linked to spoilage, when food sat too long before being eaten. Planning for who is actually coming, rather than who might come, makes the biggest difference.

Leftovers are not failures. A planned second meal, or a portion set aside on arrival, turns them into something useful.

What you can do

Practical steps for hosts, caterers and guests

Small decisions before and during an event can cut waste sharply without making the celebration feel smaller.

Confirm numbers early

Use RSVPs and a final headcount a few days out. Share it with the caterer in writing, so orders match the people who will actually attend.

Check every order

Review the order against the menu before it's confirmed. Order errors were a major source of waste in the takeaway data.

Serve in stages

Keep most food in the kitchen and refill buffet trays as needed, instead of filling everything at once. Fewer, smaller trays reduce what gets thrown away.

Plan leftovers in advance

Arrange containers, labelled and chilled, before the event. Offer guests a take-home box, so food leaves with people who want it.

Donate what's safe

Contact local food rescue or community kitchen groups to see whether surplus cooked food can be collected. Check their rules first about what they can accept.

Track the waste

For caterers and restaurants, weighing what goes in the bin for a few events shows the real cost and helps justify changes to portions and orders.

Generosity doesn't have to mean excess.

The aim is not to serve less. It is to serve well: enough for everyone, with a plan for what is left. Feeding people is at the heart of many celebrations, and so is making sure the food is eaten.
